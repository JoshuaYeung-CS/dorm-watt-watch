Your three biggest wins

1. Swap incandescent and halogen bulbs for LEDs

Replace each incandescent or halogen bulb you use in the evening with an LED of the same brightness.

CO2e uses EPA eGRID's average (total output) rate for NYUP, 242.8 lb/MWh, grossed up for 4.2% grid loss: 0.115 kg CO2e per kWh at your meter. Power saved at the margin is usually from fossil plants; with eGRID's non-baseload rate (912 lb/MWh, 0.432 kg/kWh) the top three would avoid 128 kg CO2e/yr. The average rate is the headline because it is the conservative one here.

Method

  1. Read the meter file. 365 days of interval readings summed into clock hours.
  2. Always-on load. mean hourly use on 47 days with nobody home: 42 W, 47 away days found (a day is 'away' when its mean and peak stay near the lowest days' minimum).
  3. Regression on physical drivers. On home days the hourly use above the always-on load and the lighting estimate is fitted, with non-negative least squares, to columns built from outdoor temperature (degree-hours from balance points chosen by least squares; heating 60 F), a monotone response to the day's mean temperature (non-negative ramps, so heating can only rise as it gets colder), and your own weekly activity pattern (electronics and everything else). The appliance checklist only switches parts off (no AC means no cooling term).
  4. Lighting, bottom-up. A whole-home meter cannot tell evening lights from evening electronics, so lighting = your 3 halogen bulbs x a typical 43 W x 75% on-share, in every hour the meter shows you up (use 50 Wh above the always-on load) while the sun is low at your location. Wattage and on-share are assumptions; the range shown is ±40%.
  5. Hour-by-hour shares. Each hour's metered kWh is split in proportion to the fitted parts, so the categories add up to your bill.
  6. Uncertainty. Days are resampled with replacement and the fit repeated (day-block bootstrap) for 90% intervals.
  7. Savings. Each option multiplies the estimated kWh it affects by a sourced reduction factor or a labelled assumption; money uses the EIA state residential price and CO2e uses EPA eGRID.

Accuracy

Tested on 40 synthetic student households simulated minute by minute (thermostat-driven heater and AC on real 2025 Ithaca weather, cycling fridge, randomly timed lights, gaming, TV, cooking, breaks). The savings themselves: feeding each test household's true category energy into the same savings rules, the estimated top-three saving was off by a median 4% (1 in 5 households: over 9%), and the same three changes came out on top in 75% of households. (The computer-sleep saving comes mostly from the checklist, which helps this agreement.)

Read the numbers this way: in these tests the median error was 4% for heating, 11% for the always-on load (it reads low), 19% for electronics and other plug loads and 32% for lighting (the lighting test is optimistic: the simulator and the estimator both assume lights are on when you are up after dark). The 90% sampling intervals cover only day-to-day noise and are too narrow as error bars. Savings inherit these errors.

Limits

  • A whole-home meter cannot see individual devices. Categories are estimated from when and how use changes with weather, daylight and your routine; devices that share a pattern (TV vs. PC, cooking vs. chargers) cannot be separated and are grouped.
  • A heater that runs whatever the weather looks like any other plug load; heating is identified only through its link to outdoor temperature.
  • Lighting depends on the bulb count and type you enter.
  • The fridge/standby split inside the always-on load is a prior from ENERGY STAR data, not a measurement.
  • The always-on load is measured on away days; in winter breaks a cold, empty room makes a fridge work less, so it reads about 10% low in tests.
  • Without a temperature file, heating and cooling cannot be separated from other loads; a file with only cold months cannot separate a heater's steady part from plug loads.
  • Accuracy was measured on simulated households, not on labelled real homes.
  • CO2 uses annual-average eGRID2023 rates; hour-by-hour grid intensity is not modelled. Prices are the state average, not your tariff.
  • Per-year figures scale the data period to 365 days.
